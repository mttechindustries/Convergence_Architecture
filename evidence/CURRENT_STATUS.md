## Strongest established layers

### Physical and formal mechanisms

- Pulsed radio-frequency exposure can produce microwave-auditory perception through thermoelastic transduction.
- Human respiration, cardiac micromotion, pose, movement, and activity can alter optical, radar, millimetre-wave, and Wi-Fi measurements.
- Surface EMG can capture intentionally produced silent-speech activity.
- Directional RF and high-power microwave systems require detection, tracking, waveform control, pointing, delivery, and effect assessment.

### Controlled capabilities

- Commodity Wi-Fi channel measurements have supported contactless respiration and human activity sensing in controlled research.
- Wi-Fi measurements and trained models have supported body segmentation and pose estimation without visible-light imagery as the primary sensor.
- Textile EMG headphone and neckband systems have classified limited silent-speech vocabularies.
- UAV-borne radar and UAV video have demonstrated contactless respiration or heart-related measurement.
- Distributed airborne RF elements have been evaluated as cooperative or phased apertures.
- Thermal-drone imagery has been interpreted using adapted vision-language models in ecological applications.

### Patents and named products

- United States Air Force-assigned patents describe preprocessing and apparatus for intelligible subjective speech through the RF-hearing effect.
- Areté describes AIMS as an aerial or ground image-based system for person detection, geolocation, body position, vital signs, and remote triage support.
- IntelliNet Sensors described Lynx6-A as a UAV- or robot-assisted breathing and heartbeat detector for survivor search.

### Institutional or normative records

- Public records describe fielding and testing of high-power microwave counter-UAS systems.
- International neurotechnology governance has explicitly recognized mental privacy, neural-data consent, manipulation, autonomy, and AI-neurotechnology convergence as policy concerns.
